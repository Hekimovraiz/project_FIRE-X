Project Overview

The Science Behind FIRE-X

Why Microgravity & Partial Gravity?

In microgravity (~0g), buoyancy-driven convection is absent: flames become spherical, low-temperature cool flames (600–800 K) persist after visible quenching, and low ventilation flows (5–15 cm/s) make thin materials MORE flammable than on Earth. Because there is currently no raw partial-gravity flight data in NASA PSI (pending future LUCI and FM2 lunar investigations), FIRE-X bounds Lunar (0.16g) and Martian (0.38g) hazards between 0g ISS forced-flow data and 1.0g NASA-STD-6001 Test 1 upward screening, supplemented by ACME E-FIELD electric-body-force partial-gravity mimicry.

The 569-Test Canonical PSI Dataset

569 canonical records (407 row-level records from official NASA PSI tables — FLEX PSI-69, BASS-II PSI-25, SAFFIRE-I PSI-98 — plus 162 illustrative, team-compiled records, labelled as such) across 18 NASA PSI investigation sub-suites (6 core challenge programs): FLEX, FLEX-2 & CFI (spherical droplets + CO₂ up to 70% & He up to 45% diluent suppression in ISS CIR/MDCA), BASS & BASS-II, SPICE, and SLICE (in the ISS MSG modular fan-driven flow duct), SAFFIRE-I, -II & -III (large-scale Cygnus spacecraft burns), ACME Suite (6 gaseous-fuel CIR investigations: BRE, CFI, CFI-G up to 85% O₂ inside-out flames, CLD, E-FIELD, s-Flame), and SAME, SAME-R, DAFT & DAFT-2 (smoke aging & lunar/Martian dust discrimination in ISS MSG).

Live SQLite SQL-RAG + OpenAI GPT-4o-mini

The platform pairs OpenAI GPT-4o-mini with FIRE-X's own Citation-Locked SQL-RAG technical architecture querying fire_safety.db on every prompt. Standards distinction: Citation-Locked RAG is our custom software architecture (not a NASA standard); NASA-STD-6001 governs material flammability, offgassing, and compatibility testing; and NASA-STD-3001 covers spaceflight human-system health, performance, and crew safety requirements.

Mission Simulator & Scope Exclusion

The Fire Hazard Index formula FHI = min(10, 5.1 × (X_O2/0.21)^1.5 × (P/101.3)^0.4 × M_f) models Spacecraft, Space Station (ISS), Lunar Base (0.16g), Martian Base (0.38g), Pressurized Rover (≥30% O₂, ≤10.2 psi), and EVA Spacesuit (85–100% O₂, 4.3 psi). Scope Note: Rocket engine propellants (LOX/LH₂, hypergolics) are intentionally excluded because high-pressure turbulent atomization (>50 bar, Fr ≫ 1000) is buoyancy-independent, unlike low-velocity spacecraft cabin fires.

Spaceflight & Terrestrial Impact

3-Tier Fire Safety Equipment Impact & Earth Spin-Offs

TIER 1 // FIRE PREVENTION

Material Screening & Firefighter Gear

In Space: BASS, BASS-II, ACME BRE, and SAFFIRE-I/II/III show how low forced airflow (5–15 cm/s) and elevated O₂ alter solid flammability limits, informing material screening for Artemis Lunar Bases and Pressurized Rovers.

On Earth (Spinoff 9918 & 9582): Project FIRES (1982, NASA Marshall + FEMA) transferred Apollo spacesuit thermal protection materials to firefighter attire; JSC + Scott Aviation developed 20 lb, 4,500 psi fiberglass-wrapped aluminum air tanks (13 lb lighter than conventional tanks).

TIER 2 // FIRE DETECTION

Smoke Aging & Dust Discrimination

In Space: SAME and SAME-R measured microgravity smoke particle nucleation and agglomeration, while DAFT and DAFT-2 used the same hardware to detect dust.

On Earth (Spinoff 9407): The Space Shuttle avionics Brunswick Incipient Fire Detector discriminates combustion particles from background dust, cutting nuisance alarms in industrial facilities and underground mines.

TIER 3 // FIRE SUPPRESSION

He / CO₂ Diluents & Fine Water Mist

In Space: FLEX (PSI-69) quantified droplet extinction limits across CO₂ (up to 70%) and Helium (He, up to 45%) diluents, alongside ISS Portable Fine Water Mist (PFWM) extinguishers.

Historical Lessons (Apollo 13 & Mir 1997): Apollo 13 Oxygen Tank No. 2 ruptured at ~56h after 65V DC ground-test switch welding (1,000°F); Mir's 1997 Kvant-1 oxygen-candle fire forced 36 hours of mask use.
